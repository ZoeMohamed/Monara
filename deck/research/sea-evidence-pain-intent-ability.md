# Monara: Evidence for the Southeast Asia Personal-Finance Opportunity

## One-page summary: strongest slide-ready facts

The best evidence is strongest for **financial cushion, demand for AI-assisted money management, and digital ability to pay**. The weakest part of the case is direct willingness to pay for a personal-finance app. I did not find a credible 2023 to 2026 survey giving country-level willingness-to-pay percentages for a budgeting app across these six markets.

### Do not plot these as if they were comparable

The Philippine IPA emergency-liquidity figure is intentionally a **non-national sample of 1,000 active DFS users**, while UOB samples Mass through Affluent online consumers. IPA's **88% difficulty raising US$218 (≈ RMB 1,467.42) within a month** therefore should not be plotted directly beside UOB's country percentages for a three-month emergency fund. They measure different things in different populations. [IPA Philippines FCPS](https://poverty-action.org/sites/default/files/2026-08/Philippines-2025-Financial-Consumer-Protection-Survey-FCPS-Updated.pdf)

Likewise, the BSP Philippine budgeting figure comes from a nationally representative **n=8,784 adult sample age 15+**, unlike the UOB online samples. [BSP 2025 CFIS](https://www.bsp.gov.ph/Inclusive%20Finance/Financial%20Inclusion%20Reports%20and%20Publications/2025/2025CFISreport.pdf)

The following concepts also should never share one chart as though they were the same metric:

- “Has **any** emergency fund” is different from “can cover **at least three months**,” which is different again from “can raise a fixed emergency amount within a month.”
- “Digital tools help me **track** spending” is not equivalent to “I **keep a budget**” or “I **record every transaction**.”
- “I **trust AI**,” “I **use AI daily**,” “I **used AI for budgeting**,” and “I **want overspending alerts**” measure four different stages of adoption.
- “Uses an e-wallet” measures access and habit, not disposable income and not willingness to buy Monara.

## Pricing and willingness-to-pay readout

This is the part where the evidence needs to be presented honestly.

I did **not** verify a recent large survey saying, for example, “X% of Indonesians would pay US$1.60 (≈ RMB 10.77) per month for a budgeting app.” The same is true for Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, Vietnam and the Philippines. Any such percentage presented as established evidence would be **unverified**.

The strongest behavioral benchmark is RevenueCat's 2026 State of Subscription Apps, whose primary metric period is 2025. Its dataset contains **more than 115,000 subscription apps and more than one billion transactions**. Its IN/SEA user geography explicitly combines **India, Indonesia, Thailand, the Philippines, Vietnam, Malaysia and Singapore**, and user age is not reported. Finance apps are also not isolated in the public category taxonomy: RevenueCat groups Finance together with Weather, Reference, Utilities and Tools under “Utilities.” citeturn21view0

For users in IN/SEA, RevenueCat reports a **median monthly subscription price of US$3.75 (≈ RMB 25.24)**. The proposed Monara Premium price of **US$1.60 (≈ RMB 10.77)** is therefore \(1.60/3.75 \approx 42.7\%\) of that regional monthly benchmark. This makes US$1.60 look **plausibly low relative to existing app-store subscription pricing**, but it does not prove people see enough value in Monara specifically to subscribe. [RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps/). citeturn22view1turn22view2

RevenueCat's observed payer economics provide a second useful anchor: median realized lifetime value after one month for an IN/SEA payer is **US$10.59 (≈ RMB 71.28)**, and after one year it is **US$19.32 (≈ RMB 130.05)**. These are conditional on a person already becoming a paying customer. They therefore demonstrate **actual digital subscription expenditure**, not population-level willingness to pay. citeturn22view2

The dataset also reports a median **1.4% day-35 download-to-paid conversion rate for apps headquartered in IN/SEA**. That is a developer-HQ cut, not a country-of-user conversion rate, so it is useful only as a broad app-economy benchmark and should not be described as Monara's expected conversion. [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps/). citeturn21view0

For the slide, the defensible monetization wording is:

> **“US$1.60/month is below prevailing regional app-subscription benchmarks, while observed IN/SEA subscription data confirms that consumers already pay for digital apps. Direct willingness to pay for Monara remains to be validated.”**

That is much stronger in front of a judge than pretending a regional app benchmark is a direct Monara WTP study.

## Judge-risk wording, bottom line, and claims not to make

### Figures that could mislead a judge

**UOB's 29% to 39% digital-tracking figures are the biggest interpretation trap.** Saying “only 29% of Vietnamese track spending” would be wrong. The survey asked how digital tools help manage or track wealth. Safe wording: **“29% of Vietnamese respondents said digital tools help them track income, spending or cash flow.”** Manual spreadsheets, notebooks, bank-statement checking and other tracking are not ruled out. citeturn16view1

Similarly, UOB's emergency-fund numbers do **not** show that 89% to 93% of consumers are financially secure. In Indonesia, for example, **89% have some emergency fund but only 50% report enough for at least three months**. Safe wording: **“Half of surveyed Indonesian consumers lacked a reported buffer of at least three months,”** not “half have no emergency savings.” citeturn16view6

The Philippine IPA figure is powerful but easy to overclaim. **88%** applies to **1,000 active DFS users in a purposive market-intercept sample**, not to all Filipino adults. The safest wording is: **“In a 2025 survey of 1,000 active Filipino digital-finance users, 88% reported difficulty raising US$218 (≈ RMB 1,467.42) within a month for an emergency.”** [IPA Philippines FCPS](https://poverty-action.org/sites/default/files/2026-08/Philippines-2025-Financial-Consumer-Protection-Survey-FCPS-Updated.pdf)

Visa's **71% GenAI-banking interest** and **75% GenAI spending-tracking interest** are regional results across six markets. They are excellent product-direction evidence, but not valid as an Indonesia, Philippines or Thailand number individually. [Visa CPA 2024](https://www.visa.com.my/content/dam/VCOM/regional/ap/singapore/global-elements/documents/visa-cpa-2024-report-smt.pdf). citeturn18view2turn18view3

UOB's AI numbers also measure different things. For Vietnam, **55% daily AI financial use**, **72% trust in AI**, **76% trust in a budgeting/planning app**, **30% use of AI for budgeting/spending/saving decisions**, **42% interest in overspending alerts** and **49% interest in savings nudges** are separate questions. Do not combine them into a synthetic “AI adoption score.” citeturn16view2turn16view3turn16view4turn16view5

Mobile-wallet adoption is strong evidence for **payment infrastructure and digital comfort**, not proof of disposable income. A person can use an e-wallet frequently and still be unable or unwilling to spend **US$1.60 (≈ RMB 10.77)** each month on Monara. citeturn18view4turn5view0

RevenueCat's **US$3.75 (≈ RMB 25.24)** monthly benchmark is not a finance-app-specific price. It is an IN/SEA cross-category subscription-app benchmark, and IN/SEA includes India. Likewise, the **US$10.59 (≈ RMB 71.28)** month-1 payer value is conditional on already being a payer. Safe wording: **“regional subscription-app benchmark,”** not “what Southeast Asians are willing to pay for finance apps.” citeturn21view0turn22view2

The age fit is imperfect throughout. Monara targets **20-44**, while UOB generally covers **18-65**, Visa covers **18-65**, BSP covers **15+**, and Singapore's 2026 UOB sample extends to **80**. UOB defines Gen Y as **28-43** in 2026, which is close to the core Monara segment, but the relevant country charts are not published exclusively for that cohort. citeturn14view0

### Bottom line

The research supports the pitch most convincingly when the four claims are tightened:

**Claim 1 should become “money tracking is still fragmented and digital tracking is not universal,” not “people do not track their money.”** The five-country UOB data supports limited use of digital tools specifically for cash-flow tracking, but the Philippine BSP evidence indicates budgeting itself can be widespread. citeturn16view1

**Claim 2 is defensible as “many consumers have inadequate emergency depth,” not “people have no savings.”** Among comparable UOB respondents, only **50% in Indonesia, 63% in Malaysia, 67% in Singapore, 65% in Thailand and 59% in Vietnam** report at least three months of coverage. The Philippine active-DFS study independently shows severe difficulty mobilizing emergency cash. [UOB ACSS 2026](https://www.uob.com.sg/assets/web-resources/asean-insights/pdf/acss-2026-regional-report.pdf); n=1,000 per country, 2026. citeturn16view6

**Claim 3 is the strongest new evidence.** In the same UOB 2026 survey, trust in a financial-planning/budgeting app reaches **66% Indonesia, 64% Malaysia, 63% Singapore, 74% Thailand and 76% Vietnam**. AI is already used for budgeting/spending/saving by **37%, 35%, 29%, 33% and 30%**, respectively. Visa independently finds **75% regional interest in GenAI for tracking and analyzing spending** across the six target markets. citeturn16view3turn16view4turn18view2

**Claim 4 is defensible as “the digital rails and existing subscription behavior exist.”** UOB and Visa document widespread e-wallet/QR/mobile-banking behavior, while RevenueCat observes real paid-subscription economics across IN/SEA at price points materially above Monara's **US$1.60 (≈ RMB 10.77)** proposed price. What remains unproven is whether Monara's specific value proposition converts enough users to Premium. citeturn5view0turn18view4turn22view2

### Claims not to make

- **“Most Southeast Asians do not track their spending.”** The new comparable data does not establish that.
- **“Most Southeast Asians have no emergency fund.”** UOB actually finds **89% to 93%** have some fund across its five markets; the weakness is how long the fund lasts. citeturn16view6
- **“75% of Southeast Asians want an AI budgeting app.”** Visa's **75%** concerns interest in using GenAI for tracking/analyzing spending within a broader money-management question, not purchase intent for a standalone app. citeturn18view2
- **“71% would use Monara.”** Visa's **71%** is regional interest in trying GenAI in banking, not Monara adoption. citeturn18view2
- **“US$1.60 is proven willingness to pay.”** It is **unverified**. The RevenueCat evidence establishes a regional subscription benchmark, not Monara-specific demand.
- **“The 1.4% RevenueCat conversion rate predicts Monara's conversion.”** It is a broad IN/SEA developer-HQ benchmark across subscription apps, not a personal-finance-app forecast. citeturn21view0
- **“E-wallet adoption proves users can comfortably afford Premium.”** It proves access to digital payment methods, not discretionary purchasing power.
- **“WhatsApp, LINE, Messenger or Zalo reaches X% daily in every target market.”** **Unverified** at the sample-quality standard requested.
- **“Most users receive financial transaction emails or e-receipts.”** **Unverified** from the reviewed 2023-2026 primary sources.
